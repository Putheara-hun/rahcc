Build Order for the 10 × 20 m House

Concept idea and drawing by Chhim Putheara

The house is built from the ground up, in 16 stages. Finish each stage and pass its hold points before starting the next. Hidden work (pipes under the slab, steel in the concrete, waterproofing) cannot be checked once it is covered.

Two phasesSo the house can be paid for in two steps

Phase 1 (now, about $24,100 with economy finishes and a labour-only builder): stages 1–4, then the ground floor parts of stages 8–16. You get the complete ground floor to live in: guest bedroom used as the main bedroom, living room, dining, kitchen with laundry, and bathroom. The first floor slab is the roof for now: it is waterproofed, drains through four downpipes, and the column steel is left sticking up so the first floor can be added later. The stair goes up to a lockable metal cover.

Phase 2 (later, when you have the money): stages 5–7 and the first floor parts of stages 8–15: the first floor rooms, roof terrace and stair house, then the carport, fish pond, fence and gates, and the upgrades (ceilings, porcelain, cladding, timber door, kitchen cabinets). Why this order

Each stage carries the one after it, and each hides the one before it. Footings carry the columns, the columns carry the slabs, and the slabs carry the walls. So the concrete frame goes up first, floor by floor, and the brick walls are only infill built afterwards. The pipes under the floor and the conduits inside the slabs go in before the concrete that covers them. The roof is waterproofed early, so rain stops reaching the rooms while the inside work goes on. Finishes come last, from the top down and from wet trades to dry trades, so nothing finished gets damaged by the work above it.

If you start in January, plan to finish the roof slab before the heavy rains in June. Pour concrete in the morning, cover fresh concrete if rain is coming, and keep it wet while it cures.

This guide follows the drawing set. The structural engineer's instructions and the building permit take priority over it. Durations are typical and depend on crew size, weather and material delivery.
